Comprehensive Directory of Major Sins (Al-Kaba'ir) in Islam

In Islamic jurisprudence (Fiqh), transgressions against Divine law are categorized into Major Sins (Al-Kaba'ir) and Minor Sins (Al-Saghair). Classical scholars—most notably Imam Al-Dhahabi in Kitab Al-Kaba'ir and Ibn Hajar Al-Haytami in Az-Zawajir 'an Iqtiraf Al-Kaba'ir—defined a major sin as any act explicitly prohibited in revelation that is accompanied by a prescribed worldly punishment (Hadd), an explicit threat of punishment in the Hereafter, a divine curse (La'nah), or a declaration of God's anger.

Conditions for Sincere Repentance (Tawbah)

Islamic scholars emphasize that major sins are not automatically wiped away by general good deeds or daily prayers. For repentance from a major sin to be accepted by Allah, jurists outline four foundational criteria:

  1. Immediate Cessation: Completely stopping the sinful act immediately.
  2. Sincere Remorse: Feeling deep regret in the heart for having violated God's commands.
  3. Firm Determination: Making a resolute intention never to return to the sin in the future.
  4. Restitution of Rights (If Human Rights Were Violated): Returning stolen property, compensating financial fraud, or seeking personal forgiveness if the sin involved theft, backbiting, or physical injury against another human being (Huquq Al-'Ibad).
