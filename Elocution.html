[Introduction]

(Stand tall, smile warmly, take a breath, and look directly at the audience.)

Good morning respected judges, teachers, and my dear friends.

Imagine waking up tomorrow, stepping up to your window, and pulling back the curtains—only to see a thick grey haze covering the sky instead of bright morning sunlight. Imagine turning on the tap and hearing only a dry hiss, or visiting your favorite park only to find it piled high with plastic waste.

(Pause for 2 seconds. Look around the room.)

It sounds like a scene from a bad movie, doesn't it? But if we aren't careful, this could easily become our everyday reality.

(Small pause…)

My name is Muhammed Ajmal, and today, I am honored to talk about a topic that belongs to every single one of us: "Our planet, our responsibility."

[The Reality Check]

For thousands of years, Earth has been the ultimate host. It gives us clean air to breathe, water to drink, lush forests, mighty mountains, and oceans brimming with life. And what does it ask for in return? Nothing. Just a little care, respect, and gratitude.

But look at how we’ve treated our home.

Every single year, millions of tons of plastic end up in our oceans. Ancient forests are chopped down to make room for concrete buildings. We pump smoke into the sky until taking a deep breath feels like a health risk. We act as if we have a backup Earth tucked away in our backpacks! But the reality is simple—there is no Planet B.

[Why It’s OUR Responsibility]

(Speak directly and warmly to the judges and audience, using natural hand gestures.)

Now, someone my age might easily say: "Wait a minute! I’m only in 6th grade! I don't run giant factories. I don't drive heavy trucks. Why is this my responsibility? Shouldn't the adults fix this?"

Well, here is the honest truth: adults may have created many of these problems, but we are the ones who will grow up in this world. If we wait around for someone else to step in and fix everything, it will be too late.

Responsibility doesn't start in a big government building or a high-level meeting. It starts right in our bedrooms, our school classrooms, and our local parks.

[Small Actions, Big Impact]

Taking care of the planet doesn't mean you need to invent a massive machine that cleans the ocean overnight. It means making smart, daily choices.

  • Turning off the tap while brushing your teeth saves gallons of precious water every week.
  • Switching off lights and fans when leaving a room saves energy.
  • Saying no to plastic straws or single-use bags keeps trash out of a sea turtle's ocean.
  • Planting just one small sapling on your birthday gives fresh oxygen to your neighborhood and a home to birds.

Imagine if every single student in our school did just those four simple things. That would mean thousands of saved trees, millions of gallons of clean water, and tons of plastic kept off our streets. That isn't small—that is a massive movement!

[Conclusion]

(Lower your voice slightly for emphasis, then build up to a strong, confident finish.)

There is a famous proverb that says: "We do not inherit the Earth from our ancestors; we borrow it from our children." Right now, we are returning a borrowed home that is damaged and bruised. But it doesn't have to stay that way.

Let us make a promise today. Let’s promise that we won't just talk about saving the environment in speeches—we will actually live it. Let’s be the generation that stopped making excuses and started making a difference.

Because Earth isn't just a place we live on—it is a wonderful home we belong to.

Our planet is, and will always be, OUR responsibility.

Thank you!
